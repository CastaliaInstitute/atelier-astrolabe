# Astrolabe Marketing Strategy

**Working plan — July 2026**

## Executive decision

Launch Astrolabe as a **humane AI pocketwatch**, not as a general-purpose AI
assistant or smartwatch. Its distinctive promise is:

> Notice time. Capture what matters. Ask AI when you choose.

The first commercial objective is a Kickstarter campaign for a small,
deliverable production run. Marketing should build a qualified audience before
the campaign, fund quickly from that audience on launch day, and turn the
campaign into a durable community rather than a one-time preorder event.

The product story rests on three ideas:

1. **Meaningful time** — beautiful, glanceable faces for time, moon, schedule,
   personal rhythms, and symbolic reflection.
2. **Intentional intelligence** — push-to-talk voice and Commonplace capture,
   with synthesis after the human observation rather than constant AI
   interruption.
3. **Visible privacy** — useful offline behavior, local-first personal data,
   an unmistakable microphone action, and plain disclosure when a cloud service
   is used.

## Business objective and launch gates

The campaign is validation for a focused physical product, not permission to
promise the entire firmware roadmap.

### Recommended launch objective

- Fund a first run of 500–1,000 complete devices.
- Establish an owned list of at least 5,000 qualified prospects.
- Enter launch with enough committed buyers to reach the funding goal in the
  first 24–48 hours.
- Validate a standard selling price near **$189–$229**, with a limited early
  tier near **$159–$179**.
- Avoid making a subscription necessary for ordinary ownership.

### Do not launch until

- Three representative enclosure prototypes exist and can survive repeated
  demonstrations.
- The six-minute hero flow succeeds at least 19 times out of 20.
- Battery life has been measured in named, reproducible usage scenarios.
- BOM, assembly, packaging, freight, duties, returns, support, platform fees,
  payment fees, and a contingency reserve are included in the unit economics.
- Quotes exist at 100, 500, and 1,000 units, with a backup source for critical
  components.
- A pilot group has used the device for at least three weeks.
- The team can state exactly what works offline, what needs Wi-Fi, and what
  sends data to Castalia or another service.

## Audience strategy

### Primary: intentional-technology early adopters

People who like unusual, beautifully designed tools but are uneasy with
always-listening AI. They buy e-ink notebooks, mechanical keyboards, unusual
watches, open hardware, and focused writing tools.

**Their problem:** phones and general assistants are distracting, invasive, and
generic.

**Their reason to believe:** Astrolabe is activated deliberately, has a visible
screen state, retains meaningful offline functions, and already exists as
working hardware.

### Secondary: journal and personal-knowledge practitioners

Commonplace-book users, writers, researchers, artists, and reflective-tool
enthusiasts.

**Their problem:** valuable thoughts disappear before they reach the notebook,
while voice memo archives become unusable.

**Lead use case:** press, speak, save; review or synthesize later.

### Secondary: symbolic-time and astrology enthusiasts

People drawn to lunar, astrological, seasonal, cycle, and family-rhythm views.

**Their problem:** most tools are phone apps designed for engagement rather than
calm daily use.

**Lead use case:** a beautiful daily card or rhythm face that remains useful
without opening an app.

### Tertiary: makers and developers

ESP32, open-hardware, home-automation, and experimental-interface communities.

**Their role:** technically credible early advocates and buyers of a developer
edition. Do not let developer messaging dominate the consumer campaign page.

## Positioning

### Category

**Humane AI pocketwatch**

“AI wearable” is useful for discovery but too broad to own. “Pocketwatch” makes
the form and ritual memorable. “Humane” distinguishes intentional interaction
from ambient surveillance.

### Positioning statement

For thoughtful people who want technology to support attention rather than
consume it, Astrolabe is a pocket-sized timepiece and reflection instrument
that captures thoughts and consults AI only when invited. Unlike smartwatches,
AI pins, and recording pendants, it is built around meaningful glances,
push-to-talk intent, and useful local behavior.

### Message hierarchy

1. **A pocketwatch for more than clock time.**
2. **Capture a thought without opening your phone.**
3. **Ask for help; never be silently overheard.**
4. **Your rhythms and private settings stay local by default.**
5. **A growing collection of purposeful faces.**

### Words to favor

- pocketwatch
- instrument
- intentional
- glanceable
- local-first
- push-to-talk
- Commonplace
- personal rhythms
- working prototype

### Claims to avoid

- “Your second brain”
- “Knows everything about you”
- “Replaces your phone”
- “The world’s first,” unless independently supportable
- medical, diagnostic, mind-reading, or emotion-detection claims
- unlimited or lifetime AI promises without a fully costed mechanism
- accuracy claims that cannot be reproduced publicly

## Offer architecture

Final prices must follow the production budget, but this is a useful testing
structure:

| Tier | Target price | Purpose |
|---|---:|---|
| Signal supporter | $5–$10 | Campaign journal, digital field guide, name on supporter page |
| Developer edition | $119–$149 | Board or simplified enclosure, documentation, community access |
| Super early Astrolabe | $159–$179 | Limited launch-day quantity |
| Kickstarter Astrolabe | $189–$209 | Core complete product |
| Standard Astrolabe | $219–$229 | Anchor price after limited tiers |
| Founder edition | $269–$329 | Numbered finish, distinctive case or stand, founder artifact |
| Studio bundle | $499–$649 | Three devices for makers, families, or research studios |

Principles:

- The device remains a useful clock, local instrument, and capture tool without
  a subscription.
- Include a clearly bounded introductory cloud allowance if voice synthesis has
  recurring cost.
- Make service pricing transparent before the campaign ends.
- Limit variants. One core enclosure, two finishes, and one developer option are
  enough for the first run.
- Treat stretch goals as production improvements, documentation, or a small
  number of already-developed faces—not new hardware systems.

## Funnel

### 1. Discovery

Use short, tactile demonstrations rather than abstract AI commentary.

Core content series:

- **One face, one ritual:** 15–30 second demonstrations of a single purposeful
  face.
- **Made in public:** enclosure, firmware, battery, audio, and device-test clips.
- **What it refuses to do:** concise privacy and humane-interface principles.
- **Pocketwatch field notes:** founder essays about time, attention, memory, and
  physical interfaces.
- **Unedited proof:** real latency, real boot, offline mode, failures, and fixes.

Priority channels:

1. YouTube for durable demonstrations and founder explanations.
2. Instagram/TikTok/Reels for visual discovery.
3. Reddit and specialist forums for technical credibility and feedback.
4. Email for conversion.
5. Kickstarter prelaunch page for platform followers.
6. Press, newsletters, podcasts, and creators serving intentional-tech,
   journaling, watches, astrology, and maker audiences.

### 2. Consideration

The landing page should answer, in order:

1. What is it?
2. What can I do with it in thirty seconds?
3. Is the prototype real?
4. Is it recording me?
5. What works without the cloud?
6. How much will it cost?
7. When can it ship?
8. Can this team manufacture it?

Use a two-step signup:

- Email for launch notification.
- Optional one-question segmentation: “What would you use Astrolabe for most?”
  Choices: meaningful time, capturing thoughts, personal rhythms, voice help,
  making/development.

Send each subscriber to the corresponding proof clip after signup.

### 3. Commitment

Four to six weeks before launch, ask qualified subscribers for a small,
refundable reservation deposit only if the operational and legal mechanism is
simple and transparent. Otherwise use a “Day-one backer” commitment survey.

Track committed buyers, not total followers, as the primary readiness metric.
The campaign should not launch until committed demand can plausibly cover at
least 30–40% of the funding target.

### 4. Campaign conversion

Launch-day sequence:

- Founder email at launch.
- Personal outreach to pilot users and committed prospects.
- Premier the unedited hero demonstration.
- Publish privacy architecture and manufacturing plan concurrently.
- Hold a live demonstration and questions session within 12 hours.
- Ask early backers to share a specific use case, not a generic campaign link.

During the middle of the campaign, emphasize evidence:

- pilot-user stories
- battery and durability results
- supplier and production milestones
- face demonstrations selected by audience interest
- direct answers to recurring objections

Reserve the final 72 hours for urgency, press recaps, referral prompts, and a
clear statement of what will happen after funding.

### 5. Retention and advocacy

- Publish a short update every two weeks after funding, even when the update is
  “testing continues.”
- Maintain a public fulfillment dashboard with completed, active, and at-risk
  milestones.
- Give pilot users and backers a structured route for proposing faces without
  promising that every idea will ship.
- Turn production tests into evidence-rich content.
- After delivery, organize a 30-day “Astrolabe practice” around one meaningful
  glance and one captured thought per day.

## Campaign creative

### Campaign title

**Astrolabe — The Humane AI Pocketwatch**

### Subtitle

**Meaningful time, intentional voice, and personal reflection—without an
always-listening device.**

### Hero video: 90 seconds

1. **0–10 seconds:** Phone distraction and ambient-AI fatigue; introduce the
   round object in hand.
2. **10–30 seconds:** Swipe through time, moon, schedule, and rhythm faces.
3. **30–45 seconds:** Hold, speak a thought, release, and show it saved.
4. **45–60 seconds:** Ask for an agenda or short reflection; show actual latency.
5. **60–72 seconds:** Disconnect Wi-Fi and demonstrate local behavior.
6. **72–82 seconds:** Show real prototypes, bench testing, and assembly.
7. **82–90 seconds:** Founder invitation and the central promise.

Avoid simulated interactions in the hero sequence. Any explanatory animation
should be visibly distinguished from live product footage.

### Campaign page sequence

1. Promise and hero film
2. Five immediate use cases
3. How intentional voice works
4. What works locally versus online
5. The faces included at delivery
6. Physical specifications and measured battery scenarios
7. Privacy and data-flow diagram
8. Prototype and test evidence
9. Manufacturing and fulfillment plan
10. Timeline
11. Team and relevant experience
12. Risks, service costs, and honest limitations
13. Rewards
14. Frequently asked questions

## Press and creator outreach

Build small, relevant lists rather than buying broad coverage.

### Story angles

- “An AI wearable that only listens when you hold the button”
- “The return of the pocketwatch as a personal-computing form”
- “Why this AI device deliberately does less”
- “A working ESP32 instrument for meaningful time”
- “Human observation before machine synthesis”

### Outreach groups

- intentional and calm-technology writers
- independent watch and industrial-design channels
- journaling and PKM creators
- privacy and local-first technology publications
- ESP32 and open-hardware creators
- astrology and lunar-practice educators with responsible claims
- accessible-interface and voice-interface researchers

Offer reviewers a real prototype or live remote demonstration. Do not require
positive coverage, script their conclusions, or hide limitations.

## 90-day prelaunch plan

### Days 1–30: establish the proof

- Freeze the campaign’s core feature set.
- Record the first unedited product demonstration.
- Publish the landing page and email segmentation.
- Recruit 15–25 pilot users across the primary audience groups.
- Complete pricing interviews using concrete tier choices.
- Begin weekly “One face, one ritual” posts.
- Prepare the privacy page, offline/online matrix, and public limitations.

**Exit target:** 1,000 qualified emails, 50 detailed interviews or surveys, and
a hero flow reliable enough for live demonstration.

### Days 31–60: build conviction

- Open the Kickstarter prelaunch page.
- Publish pilot diaries and measured battery results.
- Release two deeper founder videos.
- Send prototypes to a small first wave of reviewers.
- Test the title, thumbnail, first campaign paragraph, and price anchors.
- Obtain final manufacturing and fulfillment quotes.
- Run a live demonstration every two weeks.

**Exit target:** 3,000 qualified emails, 1,000 Kickstarter followers, 250–400
credible day-one commitments, and confirmed contribution margin.

### Days 61–90: convert readiness into launch

- Lock rewards, campaign copy, shipping assumptions, and production timeline.
- Publish the manufacturing plan and prototype evidence.
- Brief press and creators under agreed publication timing where appropriate.
- Rehearse launch-day support, comments, payment questions, and failure cases.
- Send a seven-day, three-day, tomorrow, and live email sequence.
- Schedule the launch demonstration and first campaign update.

**Launch gate:** committed demand covers 30–40% of the goal, the team can fund
delivery at exactly 100%, and no core demonstration relies on a simulated
feature.

## Email program

Keep emails personal, specific, and useful.

1. **Welcome:** the central promise and the one demonstration matching the
   subscriber’s selected use case.
2. **Origin:** why a pocketwatch and why push-to-talk.
3. **Proof:** unedited capture and retrieval demonstration.
4. **Privacy:** local/online matrix in plain language.
5. **Practice:** a short ritual for noticing time or capturing a thought.
6. **Making:** prototype, battery, enclosure, and test progress.
7. **Offer:** expected price range and what is included.
8. **Launch sequence:** date, early tier, live event, and direct campaign link.

## Measurement

### North-star prelaunch metric

**Number of people who explicitly intend to back at the expected price.**

### Supporting metrics

| Stage | Metric | Working target |
|---|---|---:|
| Landing page | Visitor-to-email conversion | 12–20% warm; 5–10% cold |
| Email | Welcome open rate | 50%+ |
| Email | Average click rate | 8%+ |
| Intent | Price-qualified commitments | 250–400 minimum before launch |
| Kickstarter | Prelaunch followers | 1,000+ |
| Launch | Goal funded | Within 24–48 hours |
| Campaign | Email-to-backer conversion | 5–10% overall; higher for committed list |
| Campaign | Refund/cancellation rate | Under 5% |
| Community | Update engagement | Stable through fulfillment |

Targets are hypotheses until traffic source, goal, price, and manufacturing
economics are known. Report performance by audience and source; aggregate
follower totals can hide poor purchase intent.

## Budget allocation

Do not scale paid acquisition until the organic landing page and price message
convert.

Suggested share of the prelaunch marketing budget:

- 25% prototype units and reviewer logistics
- 20% photography and live-action video
- 15% landing page, email, analytics, and campaign assets
- 15% small creator collaborations and specialist sponsorships
- 10% paid creative testing and retargeting
- 10% press samples, events, and live demonstrations
- 5% contingency

Keep manufacturing contingency in the product budget, separate from marketing.
